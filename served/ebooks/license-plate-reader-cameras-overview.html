Flock license plate reader (LPR) cameras, also called license plate recognition cameras, are designed for outdoor use to capture license plates and vehicle details, helping law enforcement quickly find vehicles relevant to an investigation.

According to the National Insurance Crime Bureau, nearly 660,000 vehicles were reported stolen across the U.S. in 2025, an average of one every 48 seconds.

The Council on Criminal Justice describes motor vehicle theft as a "keystone crime" because stolen vehicles are often used to facilitate other, more serious offenses.

License plate reader cameras provide searchable vehicle evidence that helps investigators identify leads, connect incidents, and support investigations.

Why Is License Plate Recognition Technology Important?

  • License plate recognition technology is important because in many cases, a license plate number is a crucial piece of evidence law enforcement can use to pursue an investigation. A license plate is objective, actionable, and discrete.
  • Alerting police when a stolen or known suspect vehicle enters a community can help law enforcement respond faster and potentially prevent further harm.

License plate recognition continues to play a critical role in the national crime and safety conversation. This is especially true as communities, businesses, and public safety institutions increasingly rely on technology to generate leads, support investigations, and deter crime.

What Is the Role of Vehicle Data in an Investigation?

Every investigation starts with what investigators know. Sometimes that's a witness statement or video camera footage. Often, it's a vehicle.

Vehicle data can help investigators establish where a vehicle was, when it was there, and whether it may be connected to other incidents. A license plate provides a searchable lead that can be used to identify a vehicle, check whether it has been reported stolen, and connect activity across locations and jurisdictions.

When a license plate isn't available, additional vehicle details such as make, model, color, body style, or distinguishing features can still help move an investigation forward. Combined with other evidence, vehicle data gives investigators more ways to develop leads, verify information, and build a clearer picture of what happened.

How Do License Plate Recognition Cameras Enhance Security?

License plate recognition cameras enhance security by providing actionable evidence to share with authorities.

Searchable vehicle data can include plate numbers, missing or covered plates, vehicle make, color, alterations, and other identifying information. With traditional cameras, teams may have to sift through hours of footage to find the relevant incident.

Private customers, such as businesses and HOAs, have the option to integrate their LPRs with local law enforcement to identify vehicles involved in crimes. If the customer chooses to share LPR data with law enforcement, the system can send alerts to designated law enforcement partners when it detects a flagged license plate, in accordance with the customer’s approved sharing settings.

Objective vehicle evidence can help mitigate bias and give law enforcement clearer information to support an investigation.

The benefits of license plate recognition technology extend to many individuals within a community, from law enforcement and businesses to neighborhoods, schools, and beyond.

License Plate Recognition Cameras for Neighborhoods, Gated Communities, and HOAs

Homeowners associations (HOAs) responsible for providing security services for neighborhood spaces must review and evaluate a wide array of options for securing their communities.

Communities often decide between traditional security options such as gates, lighting, and patrols. Many residents already use home security tools like smart doorbells, cameras, alarm systems, and motion sensor-activated outdoor lighting.

As a supplement to these traditional security measures, license plate recognition cameras can fill any security gaps and bolster evidence in the event of a crime. They also help reduce risk to residents, security personnel, and neighborhood watch volunteers by limiting the need for face-to-face confrontation and providing around-the-clock visibility at key entry and exit points.

Why Should I Choose Flock LPR for My Neighborhood?

Flock camera systems are designed for simple deployment through a leased model, which includes installation, maintenance, and support.

Flock LPRs primarily run on LTE and solar power, which can reduce the need for electrical work, Wi-Fi, or other infrastructure. Their small size allows them to blend in with any neighborhood aesthetic.

Your HOA or community, not Flock, controls the encrypted data collected. By default, it is retained for 7 days.

Learn more about how Flock can help your neighborhood work with law enforcement to support investigations, strengthen neighborhood security, and give residents greater peace of mind.

Why Should I Choose Flock LPR for My Business?

Retail theft has reached critical levels across the United States, according to the National Retail Federation’s 2025 Impact of Retail Theft & Violence Report.

Theft is a growing issue for retailers of all sizes. Between 2023 and 2024, retailers reported an 18% increase in shoplifting incidents and a 12% rise in merchandise theft, according to the same report. Businesses may struggle to deter thefts and identify investigative leads after the fact, even with in-store video footage.

Violence tied to theft is also on the rise. The report found a 17% jump in threats or acts of violence associated with shoplifting and theft, as well as a 16% increase in incidents involving weapons. Nearly three-quarters of respondents said shoplifters are exhibiting higher levels of aggression and hostility than in previous years.

A license plate recognition camera can help a business link a suspect to a vehicle, giving security personnel and law enforcement a stronger lead for investigation.

Incidents also happen outside businesses, such as parking lot smash-and-grabs, where LPR cameras can help deter activity and support investigations. If a vehicle on a customer-defined hotlist enters the premises, LPR cameras can alert administrators, enabling them to respond in accordance with their security protocols.

The benefits of Flock LPR for businesses include:

  • Cameras run on solar power and LTE for easy installation almost anywhere on a property, with no additional utility bills or IT integration.
  • Ability to place at-risk vehicles on custom hotlists to get automatic alerts when they drive by cameras.
  • Easily search vehicle data based on time, location, and specific details like color or body type.
  • Option to share evidence and alerts from your cameras with local law enforcement so they can act quickly if an incident occurs.

Learn more about how Flock can help your business support crime reduction efforts, improve customer experience, and strengthen employee safety.

License Plate Recognition Cameras for Apartment Complexes

If you’re managing apartments or multifamily properties, resident safety is a top priority for retention and reputation. Crime and a high rate of resident turnover can be extremely costly.

Property managers have to juggle various responsibilities with multiple units or properties. Implementing license plate recognition cameras can help deter crime, document incidents, and give teams useful evidence to support resident safety.

Learn more about how Flock helps support resident safety, satisfaction, and retention while strengthening property security and protecting your investment.

Success With Flock LPR

Flock license plate recognition camera systems help communities gather objective evidence, support investigations, and strengthen public safety efforts across the United States. Flock is present in 49 states and thousands of cities.

San Francisco, California, uses most of Flock's technology, including Flock LPR. In 2025, crime fell 25 percent in a single year. Homicides hit their lowest level in 70 years.

In Pinecrest, Florida, the Pinecrest Police Department used Flock LPR to quickly identify the suspect vehicle linked to a home invasion and a series of vehicle thefts, helping officers coordinate a response that led to the arrest of three suspects. The case demonstrates how real-time investigative technology can help law enforcement develop leads and act quickly during active investigations.

In Alameda County, California, the Alameda County Sheriff’s Office used Flock license plate readers to help solve a home invasion.

“Without the license plate reader technology, we would not have been able to identify that car,” said Alameda County Sheriff’s Office Sgt. Fenton Culley.

Alameda County residents held a press conference to demand that the unincorporated communities be provided with the same proven crime-fighting technology already used by incorporated cities across Alameda County.

Whether you’re a business security leader, HOA board member, property manager, or law enforcement officer, Flock can help strengthen security operations and support crime-reduction efforts.

FAQs About Flock LPR

Q: What do Flock license plate recognition cameras capture?

A: Flock license plate recognition cameras are designed for outdoor use to capture license plates and vehicle details. The data can include plate numbers, missing or covered plates, vehicle make, color, alterations, time, and location, making it searchable for security and investigative purposes.

Q: How do LPR cameras help support investigations?

A: LPR cameras help turn vehicle activity into searchable, objective evidence. Instead of reviewing hours of traditional video, authorized users can search by time, location, plate number, or vehicle characteristics to find vehicles relevant to an incident.

Q: Are Flock LPR cameras a good fit for HOAs, neighborhoods, businesses, and apartment communities?

A: Yes. Flock LPR cameras are commonly positioned as a security layer for neighborhoods, gated communities, HOAs, businesses, and multifamily properties where vehicle activity is relevant to safety or investigations. They are especially useful at entry points, exits, parking areas, and other locations where vehicle data can help document incidents.

Q: Can private organizations share Flock LPR data with law enforcement?

A: Private customers such as businesses and HOAs can choose whether to share LPR data or alerts with local law enforcement. If enabled, the system can notify authorities when a flagged license plate is detected, without requiring action from the camera owner.
