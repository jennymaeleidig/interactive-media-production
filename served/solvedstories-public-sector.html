The Okaloosa County Sheriff’s Office arrested Gillum at a hotel, recovering a handgun and approximately 200 rounds of ammunition from his room.

Gillum was taken into custody on a charge of making terroristic threats sought by Louisiana authorities. Information obtained by the Sheriff’s Office indicated Gillum planned a mass shooting in New Orleans, followed by “suicide by cop,” and had expressed interest in “harming black people.”

His arrest occurred just before the start of the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival (Jazz Fest), which draws 400,000 attendees. Organizers issued a statement thanking law enforcement for protecting the community.

The investigation began when Gillum’s family reported him missing in North Carolina due to a history of self-harm, possession of a Glock, and expressing interest in harming African-American people. An official from the Alamance County Sheriff’s Office said Gillum fled the state before police could secure paperwork for his involuntary psychiatric commitment.

The Okaloosa County Sheriff’s Office used the Flock LPR system to locate Gillum after receiving intelligence of his presence. Upon apprehension, deputies recovered the handgun and ammunition from his hotel room. “This disturbing case highlights how technology like FLOCK [cameras] and strong partnerships between agencies can help prevent potential violence and bring wanted fugitives into custody safely before a tragedy could occur,” the Okaloosa County sheriff said.

Gillum is being held in the Okaloosa County Jail pending extradition to Louisiana. Louisiana State Police and the FBI are investigating, noting there are "no known direct threats to any festivals in Louisiana" at this time.

Multi-Geo Search Helps Cracks Multi-County Child Molestation Case, Protecting Vulnerable Victims

Cobb County investigators leveraged Flock’s Multi-Geo Search functionality to identify a suspect linked to a child molestation investigation spanning Cobb and Douglas counties. The case, involving at least nine child victims, required seamless multi-agency collaboration to track activity along the Thornton Road corridor. This effort was further bolstered by community outreach, with authorities distributing flyers to businesses and apartment complexes to support the search strategy.

The suspect was apprehended before additional harm could occur; at the time of his arrest, he was traveling to another county, allegedly to victimize another child. He is currently held without bond as the investigation continues with potential for further charges. This case serves as a powerful example of how Flock’s cross-jurisdictional capabilities enable investigators to identify suspects faster and support time-sensitive decisions to protect vulnerable community members.

LPR Hot List Alert Triggers Incident, Flock DFR Drone Locates and Captures Second Fleeing Suspect

On April 5, shortly after 6:45 p.m., Castle Rock Police officers were alerted to a vehicle entering town with a stolen license plate via a Flock hot list alert. Officers located the vehicle and attempted a traffic stop; however, the driver failed to yield and fled at a high rate of speed, eventually entering a dead-end neighborhood. Officers did not initiate a pursuit and instead waited for the vehicle to exit the area, at which point a tire deflation device was successfully deployed.

Despite not being actively pursued, the vehicle continued to elude officers and entered Interstate 25. The vehicle later became inoperable and came to a stop on the interstate. Both occupants fled on foot across the highway, during which one suspect was struck by a vehicle and sustained life-threatening injuries. Officers on scene rendered aid until fire personnel arrived and transported the individual to a local hospital.

The second suspect fled into a nearby neighborhood and attempted to hide in bushes at a residence. The suspect was located using a Flock DFR drone and safely taken into custody.

Both suspects were found to have felony warrants for their arrest.

Flock Technology, Dispatcher Skill Lead to Stolen Truck Recovery in an Hour

Lake County Sheriff’s 911 Dispatchers played a pivotal role in the safe and rapid recovery of a stolen Ford pickup truck. After receiving a report of the theft from a gas station in Beach Park, dispatchers immediately began gathering crucial information. Demonstrating excellent use of the Flock Safety license plate reader system, the dispatchers were able to successfully search for and locate the stolen vehicle using only the make and model, as the caller did not know the license plate number. This diligence allowed them to track the grey Ford pickup truck as it passed through several Flock cameras.

The dispatchers' continued coordination with law enforcement proved essential to the successful conclusion of the case. They issued a broadcast to regional agencies and provided ongoing updates as the vehicle traveled southbound. Within an hour, officers with the Northfield Police Department located the stolen vehicle, which led to its recovery and the apprehension of the offender. Sheriff John D. Idleburg credited his team's "professionalism and skill," noting that their "ability to quickly gather critical information, leverage technology, and coordinate with officers in the field played a key role in the safe recovery of the stolen vehicle and the apprehension of the offender within an hour of the vehicle being stolen.

Flock LPR and Interagency Takedown End Weeks-Long Crime Spree

A joint operation between the Pinecrest Police Department and the Miami-Dade Sheriff’s Office used Flock LPR alerts, air support, and coordinated ground units to locate and apprehend three individuals responsible for weeks of luxury auto thefts and high-end residential burglaries across multiple jurisdictions. The seamless communication between agencies turned a single LPR hit into a regional takedown, ending the crime spree in one morning and demonstrating the power of strong interagency relationships.

“When Flock LPRs in Pinecrest alerted that the subject vehicle was repeatedly circling Pinecrest neighborhoods, One call to the Sheriff’s Office command personnel and within minutes an air unit arrived overhead, spotted the vehicle, and followed it until ground units could converge”, Chief Cohen shared on LinkedIn

Chief Cohen stated without the collaboration between the agencies and the Flock cameras, these suspects would have gone unapprehended and he could not praise the technology enough.

Warner Robins PD, Homicide Suspect Apprehended (Feb 10)

In the morning of February 10, 2026 there was a homicide that occurred on the campus of Palmetto Elementary School in Fulton County, GA. The incident was a domestic matter that came to its culmination at this location. The suspect vehicle was identified using the Flock LPRs on the campus of Palmetto Elementary School, which are part of the Fulton County School system’s Flock network. The suspect and vehicle information were relayed by officers from the incident scene.

The suspect was last seen traveling South and had ties to Warner Robins. Lt. Bish made contact with the officers manning the Real Time Crime Center (RTCC), Ofc. Nix, Ofc. Richards and Ofc. Houck. Ofc. Nix entered the tag into the Flock camera system and set it with an alert for any camera in Houston County. At about 1320 hours, Ofc. Nix broadcast over the radio the suspect vehicle had hit the Flock Camera on Russell Pkwy at Hwy 41 moving eastbound.

Squad 4 officers began moving to the area in order to intercept the suspect vehicle. Ofc. Lane observed the vehicle as it crossed Corder Rd. travelling east on Russell Pkwy. Ofc. Lane caught up to the vehicle and activated his emergency lights. The suspect vehicle accelerated and failed to stop. At this point the following was known:

  1. Suspect committed a homicide less than 2 hours previous
  2. Homicide is a forcible felony
  3. Suspect was possibly armed


Lt. Bish advised Ofc. Lane to pursue the suspect. The pursuit started at 1324 hours, on Russell Pkwy near the intersection of Burns Dr. The Houston County Sheriff’s Office was behind Ofc. Lane. When the pursuit was initiated, the deputy passed Ofc. Lane and took over as the lead vehicle on the pursuit. Lt. Bish advised the Sheriff’s Office to be the lead. WRPD officers continued on, in case the vehicle crashed or was pitted. When the vehicle turned south on Hwy 247, Lt. Bish advised units to go into service. Dispatch advised spike strips had been set up, Lt. Bish advised 2 units to continue on in case they were successful and the suspect ran. The suspect continued on and got on Hwy 96 traveling east toward Twiggs County. All WRPD units went into service.

Houston County Sheriff’s Office advised over the radio the suspect had crashed just past the creek on Hwy 96. Lt. Bish responded to that location. Lt. Bish met with the supervision of the Houston County Sheriff’s Office and ensured they did not need any assistance. Authorities discovered there was a small child inside the car. There had been no information passed on about the possibility of a child being in the car. The child was uninjured. There is no other information regarding the child. The incident was turned over to Houston County.

Greenville MS PD - Safe City - Gunshot detection (Dec 12)

On Friday, December 12, 2025, around 8am, officers with Greenville Police Department received an alert from the FLOCK Gunshot Alert Detection that showed there were multiple gunshots around Ashburn and Kirkland Streets. As officers were approaching the area, they began receiving calls from citizens that a black male occupying a black SUV was shooting in the area. A quick response from the officers led to them locating the SUV and making an arrest for the shooting. The FLOCK Safety Gunshot Detection System alerted police and dispatchers in sufficient time to the approximate area, along with calls from the community, that helped officers make an arrest that quickly closed out a case.

This is one of many recent cases that FLOCK Safety has helped law enforcement solve since its inception. We still ask the community that if you SEE SOMETHING, SAY SOMETHING. We all have a responsibility in keeping our community safe.”

The Greenville Police Department has arrested an individual of Greenville, who currently sits in the Washington County Regional Correctional Facility on a charge of Possession of Firearm by a Convicted Felon, along with several other misdemeanor charges.

Shelby Township MI PD - Double homicide solved using LPR (Nov 11)

A 46-year-old Houston man has been charged in the November 7 double homicide at Star Auto Sales in Shelby Township (MI), where the Business owner and a customer were fatally shot inside the dealership. Investigators said the killings stemmed from a dispute over a $3,000 Hummer purchase, which the suspect had previously argued about with the owner. Investigators credited Flock with a major breakthrough after it captured the getaway vehicle plate. Detectives quickly identified that the suspect was in a Turo rental vehicle and coordinated with regional agencies to track his movements. Within hours, the suspect was located and arrested at a Romulus motel, where police recovered firearms, stolen cash (from the business), and clothing matching the shooter in surveillance footage. He now faces multiple felony charges, including two counts of first-degree premeditated murder, and remains held without bond as authorities praised the rapid investigative collaboration and use of Flock data in bringing the case to a swift resolution.

Chief Shelide emphasized that the Flock cameras have been instrumental in their investigative success, praising their effectiveness in this case. He also commended the outstanding teamwork among agencies and expressed gratitude for the continued support of the Board of Trustees. “I have been doing this for 39 years now and I don’t think I’ve ever been more proud of my staff than I am right now . I‘d be remiss if I didn’t mention our Board of Trustees. They’ve given us everything we’ve asked for. We have 50 Flock cameras around the Township to keep our residents safe. Our residents want our Flock cameras and so do our business owners. We’ve solved countless crimes with these cameras."
